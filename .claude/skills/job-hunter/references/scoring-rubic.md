# Scoring Rubric

Use this when a role is borderline between two scores, or when you need to justify a score to the user.

## The 5-point scale

### 5 — Strong fit (apply today)
- Job title is a direct match or near-synonym for what the user is targeting
- 80%+ of "required" qualifications map clearly to resume bullets
- No major dealbreakers (location, seniority mismatch, missing certifications)
- The user could write the cover letter in 20 minutes because the story writes itself

**Example**: Resume shows 6 months shipping a production multi-AI mobile app with Claude Code as pair-programmer. Role is "Senior AI Engineer — agentic workflows, production LLM systems." → Score 5.

### 4 — Good fit (apply this week)
- Title matches user's target, possibly one rung off
- 60-80% of requirements met
- Gaps are explainable: adjacent stack, transferable experience, or a "nice to have" the user lacks
- Cover letter needs a paragraph explaining one or two stretch points

**Example**: Resume shows React Native + Convex. Role asks for "React Native, Firebase preferred." → Score 4. (Firebase ≠ Convex but the user can speak to backend-as-a-service patterns.)

### 3 — Stretch fit (apply if open to it)
- Adjacent role — the discipline is right but the specialty isn't an exact match
- 40-60% of requirements met
- Requires a thoughtful cover letter making the case for transferable skills
- Worth applying if the company is high-priority OR the user has stated they want stretches

**Example**: Resume is AI engineering + hardware QA background. Role is "AI Platform Engineer" requiring Kubernetes and large-scale distributed systems. → Score 3. (AI fluency matches; infra depth is the stretch.)

### 2 — Weak fit (probably skip)
- Adjacent only at a high level
- Missing one or more hard requirements (years of experience, specific framework, security clearance, in-office in a city the user isn't in)
- Cover letter would feel forced

**Example**: Role is "Staff ML Researcher" requiring a PhD and 8+ years publishing at NeurIPS. User has bootcamp + practitioner background. → Score 2.

### 1 — Not a fit (skip)
- Different discipline entirely
- The match is keyword-level only ("the JD mentions Python" doesn't make a data science role a fit for an AI engineer)

## Tiebreakers between 3 and 4

If you can't decide between 3 and 4, ask: "Could the user honestly claim, in the cover letter, that they've done something like this before?"
- Yes → 4
- "Sort of, with a stretch" → 3

## Tiebreakers between 4 and 5

If you can't decide between 4 and 5: "Would I be surprised if the user didn't get an interview?"
- Yes, surprised → 5
- No, it depends → 4

## What not to do

- Don't score on "vibes about the company." The score is about fit between the role and the resume, not about whether the company is exciting.
- Don't penalize for missing buzzwords if the underlying skill is clearly present in the resume.
- Don't reward for buzzword matches if the depth isn't there.
