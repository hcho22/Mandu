# Build a Digital Brain for AI-Assisted Content

A supplied Markdown guide distills an interview with [[nicholas-cole]] into a content-creation workflow.
It argues that human thinking supplies distinctive positions, experiences, and language, while AI retrieves, combines, and adapts that material.
Its “digital brain” is a content library, not a full specification for a personal agent or a requirement to train a custom language model.

## Key claims

- Commodity explanations can help beginners, but personal experiences and developed original ideas supply more distinctive inputs.
- Record explicit positions, reasons, exceptions, and real experiences before asking AI to draft in your name.
- Describe voice through observable choices such as vocabulary, sentence rhythm, formality, and evidence style.
- Reuse language the author still endorses; require unsupported personal or factual claims to be flagged rather than invented.
- Separate source creation from adaptation and distribution, and keep first-person stories attributed to the person who lived them.
- Evaluate timely and evergreen content on their intended horizons, including qualified responses and durable reuse rather than only immediate reach.
