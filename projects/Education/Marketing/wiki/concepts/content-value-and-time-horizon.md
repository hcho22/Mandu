# Content Value and Time Horizon

Assess content by what it contributes to a reusable library and the period over which it is intended to help its audience.
[[projects/Education/Marketing/wiki/sources/build-a-digital-brain-guide|The digital-brain guide]] distinguishes commodity, personality, and original content, separately from timely and timeless content.

## Key points

- Commodity content explains widely shared knowledge; beginner usefulness and originality are different dimensions.
- Personality content contributes the author's real experiences and judgments.
- Original content contributes a new idea or framing; new wording alone is not evidence of originality.
- Timely content depends on current relevance; evergreen material is intended for continued usefulness and reuse.
- Choose the intended outcome before judging performance: reach, qualified responses, inquiries, and durable reuse answer different questions.
- The guide provides no study establishing which category performs best and no universal publishing cadence.

## Synthesis

A useful [[content-source-library]] grows through new reasons, examples, distinctions, and evidence as well as through polished phrasing.
Mandu's practical interpretation is to evaluate an adaptation as a distribution output and a source piece as a knowledge contribution, rather than confusing the number of posts with learning.
No additional publication target follows from ingesting this framework.
