# Content Source Library

A content source library is a reusable collection of an author's positions, stories, reasoning, examples, and language.
[[projects/Education/Marketing/wiki/sources/build-a-digital-brain-guide|The digital-brain guide]] describes it as the input that makes AI-assisted writing specific to a person.

## Key points

- Capture what happened, the decision made, why it was made, and what changed; ordinary experiences can carry useful lessons.
- Record agreement, disagreement, conditions, and exceptions instead of asking AI to choose the author's beliefs.
- Keep source creation separate from repurposing: more variations of an existing statement do not necessarily add new knowledge.
- Distinguish collected third-party material from author-endorsed positions and language.
- The guide's “personal language model” can mean a retrievable file library; it does not establish a need for model training.

## Inside a growth repo (2026-10-14)

[[projects/Education/Marketing/wiki/sources/becoming-a-marketing-engineer|Becoming a Marketing Engineer]] places this library inside a company-wide [[growth-repo]] as the "content engine" folder: a founder voice guide, winning hooks, scripts, and notes on what performed.
Its founder content engine feeds the library from recorded customer conversations and podcasts, then turns one insight into several surfaces (posts, a short video, a landing-page line, an email angle, a calculator) and lets performance decide what to repeat.
Two differences from the 2026-09-23 guide are worth keeping.
It adds performance notes to the library, so the library records what worked as well as what the author thinks.
And it says nothing about endorsement, so [[approved-language-repurposing]] still governs whether a recorded founder remark can be published in the founder's name.

## Synthesis

The useful asset is attributable reasoning, not merely a large collection of notes.
[[approved-language-repurposing]] supplies the reuse boundary and [[content-value-and-time-horizon]] helps assess what a new piece contributes.
Mandu's interpretation: this content-focused library is narrower than an [[projects/Education/AI/AI_Wiki/wiki/concepts/ai-operating-system|AI operating system]], which also supports project tracking and other work.
The guide's advice to focus on one publishing topic does not require narrowing Mandu's all-subject learning library.
