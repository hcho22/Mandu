# Service Before Acquisition

Service before acquisition means earning revenue and permission to observe real work before trying to buy a company in that niche. The service should solve one bounded operational problem and remain worthwhile even if no acquisition follows.

## What the service must reveal

- The actual inputs, handoffs, exceptions, approvals, and client preferences.
- Where review is necessary and who carries accountability.
- Which knowledge is shared across firms and which rules are local.
- Whether owners and employees trust the operator with sensitive work.
- Whether the proposed improvement creates measurable value under real constraints.

The operator should work with multiple owners, use permissioned data, and avoid treating a paid engagement as entitlement to a future sale. A relationship can produce domain knowledge, reputation, and revenue without becoming an acquisition.

## The same ladder, ending in software (2026-10-14)

[[projects/Education/Marketing/wiki/sources/becoming-a-marketing-engineer|Becoming a Marketing Engineer]], filed in the Marketing wiki, gives a parallel ladder for selling AI-built growth systems: consult (embed for 30, 60, or 90 days and build one system), then productize one wedge for one niche, then build software after doing the work by hand for 5-10 companies.
The speaker would start with services because it avoids building something nobody wants, which is this page's reasoning with software, not an acquisition, as the end point.
His consulting rates ($5K, $10K, or $30K per month) are his figures, without supporting data.

## Synthesis

This is customer discovery with an obligation to deliver. Interviews describe a workflow; service delivery exposes its exceptions, missing data, privacy limits, review burden, and relationship dynamics. It is a business counterpart to [[projects/Education/AI/AI_Wiki/wiki/concepts/real-workflow-not-documented|The real workflow, not the documented one]].

The source recommends roughly 6 to 12 months of service work, but provides no evidence that this duration predicts acquisition success. The sequence is more defensible than the specific timeline.
