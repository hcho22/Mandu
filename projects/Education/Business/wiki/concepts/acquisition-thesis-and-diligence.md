# Acquisition Thesis and Diligence

An acquisition thesis states what kind of business to buy, why the buyer can improve it, and which facts automatically disqualify a deal. Diligence then tests that thesis across financial, legal, operational, people, technology, and regulatory evidence.

## Practical standard

- Define a narrow target by customer, work type, geography, size, and operating characteristics.
- Write rejection criteria before seeing a tempting deal.
- Value the company on current, supportable performance rather than hoped-for AI savings.
- Include debt service, working capital, taxes, insurance, professional fees, software, security, management, transition, and integration labor in the economics.
- Test customer concentration, owner dependence, employee retention, data access, contractual duties, and compliance exposure.
- Model downside cases in which automation takes longer, costs more, or produces less benefit than expected.
