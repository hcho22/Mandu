# Human-Centered Integration

Human-centered integration treats employees, managers, sellers, clients, relationships, and tacit judgment as operating assets that must survive a change of ownership.

## Practices

- Give a capable general manager clear authority and retain the founder only where their contribution is explicit.
- Use a structured seller transition to transfer relationships, history, exceptions, and unwritten judgment.
- Capture employee knowledge by observing work and reviewing cases, not by assuming every rule can be documented in advance.
- Keep early changes small and reversible; test new workflows in the background before they affect clients.
- Track employee and client retention alongside cost and productivity measures.
- Explain role changes honestly, including where automation may reduce or reshape work.

## Synthesis

The acquired company's continuity often lives in people rather than software. Removing the people who understand exceptions can erase the evidence needed to improve the system and can damage client trust before efficiencies appear.

This principle does not settle the ethical or employment consequences of automation. The source acknowledges those tensions but does not provide a workforce plan, labor analysis, or evidence about long-term outcomes.
