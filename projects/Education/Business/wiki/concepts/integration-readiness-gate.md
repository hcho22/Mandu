# Integration Readiness Gate

An integration readiness gate is a recorded decision about whether one acquired business is stable enough for the owner to add another. It prevents portfolio growth from outrunning operational control.

## Gate questions

- Are client retention and service quality stable?
- Are employees staying, and do they understand the changed workflows?
- Can the general manager operate without daily founder intervention?
- Are correction rates, review time, and human effort understood and improving?
- Are company- and client-specific rules preserved rather than overwritten by generic standards?
- Are incidents detected, escalated, and resolved through tested runbooks?
- Do fully loaded financial results support the original case?
- Does the owner have actual capacity for another integration?

Record the outcome as ready, not ready, or ready only after named conditions are met. Mixed evidence means pause and investigate rather than averaging risks into a favorable answer.

## Synthesis

The gate converts “scale after the first integration works” into an auditable management decision. It also makes stalled expansion informative: a failed condition identifies which capability needs work.

The source does not provide universal thresholds, and none should be invented. Thresholds depend on the service, client commitments, financing, and regulatory exposure.
