# Business Wiki

This wiki owns reusable knowledge about business acquisition, operations, integration, governance, and economics.
Mandu writes and maintains pages; hcho supplies sources and reviews the results.

## Conventions

- Preserve incoming source bytes in the sibling `raw/` folder; never edit a saved source.
- Use kebab-case Markdown filenames and Obsidian wikilinks.
- Use full vault-relative links between wikis and for generic filenames such as `index`, `README`, and `synthesis`.
- Source pages record `type`, `title`, `source-type`, `author`, `url`, `date-ingested`, `date-created`, and `tags`; use `unknown` or `local` when details are absent.
- Concept pages record `type`, `tags`, `status`, `source-count`, and `last-updated`.
- Keep one concept per page, created only when a source warrants it; link existing concepts in other wikis instead of duplicating them.
- Separate source claims, compiler additions, and Mandu's synthesis.
- A tutorial, market thesis, or case study does not establish investment merit or authorize a project.
- Put each full sentence on its own line, use absolute dates, and avoid em dashes in authored notes.

## Operations

For `/ingest`, read the full source and relevant existing pages, file automatically under the agreed Mandu workflow, update every affected page and the index, and append an entry to `log.md`.
Ask only about material ambiguities that prevent accurate filing; preserve competing claims and identify missing provenance.
Give a short review with links afterward.

For questions, start with the index, cite the relevant notes, and clearly distinguish source claims, outside evidence, and interpretation.
For maintenance, check broken links, orphan pages, duplicate concepts, stale claims, and source counts.
Update synthesis when a source changes the working interpretation, while avoiding false corroboration from repeated summaries of the same material.

Project status, personal preferences, and hard-to-reverse decisions remain in Mandu's operational records.
Legal, tax, accounting, financing, employment, regulatory, and investment decisions require appropriate primary evidence and qualified advice.
