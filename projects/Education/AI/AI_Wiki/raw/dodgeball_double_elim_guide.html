DODGEBALL DOUBLE-ELIMINATION RUMBLE

16 Teams · 1v1 → 4-Team Four-Square · Double-Elim + Redemption Rumble · ~75 min

Everybody plays, nobody stands still — survive the four-square or fight your way back.

Players: ~200 men Teams: 16 × 12–13 Clock: ~75 min Field: ~175 × 110 ft R1: 1v1  ·  R2: 4-team four-square Games: 18–19 total

The Big Idea (why this hits all three goals)

The tournament escalates in format, not just stakes: Round 1 is 1v1 (classic head-to-head, 8 matches, all 16 teams playing in the first 10 minutes), then Round 2 is 4-team four-square — the schoolyard game scaled up: one big square split into four quadrants, each team holds a zone, throw across the lines, last zone standing survives. That single change is a huge energy and creativity spike: choosing which neighbor to target invites temporary alliances, gang-ups, and last-second betrayals, and it collapses the field fast so the whole bracket fits comfortably in 75 minutes.

Because a 4-way sends three teams down at once, we keep it a true double-elimination: losing a 1v1 or getting knocked out of a four-square is your first life lost, not your last — you drop into the losers bracket and keep going. And the instant a team takes its second loss, it rolls onto a freed court for the continuous Redemption Rumble, so no team of 200 men ever stands idle.

The three levers. High energy = short games, four-square territory battles, and a converging center showcase for the finals. Teamwork = catch-to-revive + assigned roles reward a coordinated team, and in four-square a team that defends its zone as a unit outlasts scattered ones. Creativity = no fixed lineup, captain power-plays, four-square alliance-brokering, and rotating variant rules in the Rumble.

Field Layout — Pre-Mark BOTH Court Sets (no reconfiguring)

Mark the field once, in two colors: four 50 × 30 ft courts (2×2 grid) for the 1v1 rounds, and two ~50 × 60 ft four-square arenas that overlay the same footprint (each arena = the two courts in one column merged, then split into four quadrants by a “+”). Refs just point teams to the right color — no cones move mid-event. The center spine collapses to one showcase court for the finals.

The Bracket (visual)

Every team gets two lives. A 1v1 loss or a failure to survive a melee is one loss and drops you to the losers bracket (blue). A second loss ends your bracket run — then you’re into the Redemption Rumble. Seeds are shown in standard 1–16 order.

W Winners bracket — 11 games

  • R1 · 1v1: 8 games (all 16 teams)
  • R2 · 4-team four-square: 2 games (8 teams → 2 survive)
  • Winners Final · 1v1: 1 game

L Losers bracket — 6 games

  • LR1 · 4-square: 2 (the 8 R1 losers)
  • LR2 · 4-square: 2 (LR1 survivors + 6 four-square drops)
  • LR3 · 1v1: 1  ·  Losers Final · 1v1: 1

Grand Final: 1 game (+1 possible reset). 18–19 games total. Every team is guaranteed at least two games; the champion plays up to six.

The Bracket Master is your single point of failure — staff it well. One person owns the whiteboard, records each result the instant a ref calls it, and hands the staging marshal the next matchups. Four-square results are the trickiest to log — have the arena ref hand over a written “surviving zone + 3 out” slip every time.

Court Rules

1v1 rounds (R1, Winners Final, LR3, Losers Final, Grand Final)

  • On court: up to 12 a side; rotate the 13th in on the next game or via a catch-revive.
  • Balls: 8 foam balls on the center line, opening rush on the horn.
  • Win: eliminate all opponents, or at the 4:00 cap the team with more players standing.
  • Out: hit below the shoulders on the full, or your throw is caught.
  • Catch-to-revive: a clean catch puts the thrower out and brings a teammate back — the teamwork engine.
  • Headshots don’t count against the player hit; intentional head-hunting = thrower out. Cross the line = out.

4-team four-square rounds (Winners R2, Losers R1 & R2)

It’s the schoolyard four-square you already know, scaled up to teams with dodgeballs. One big square, split into four quadrants by a “+” of lines. Each team owns one quadrant and stays in it — the game is territorial, not a pile-up in the middle.

  • Stay in your quadrant. Field up to ~8–10 per team in each zone; rotate the rest. Step across your own line and you’re out (same rule as crossing center in a 1v1).
  • Throw across the lines into any of the other three zones — sides or diagonal. Any ball that comes to rest in your quadrant is yours to pick up and throw. Start with ~12 balls spread across the whole square.
  • Out: hit below the shoulders on the full, or your throw is caught. Same catch-to-revive as the 1v1 games (catch → thrower out + one teammate back). Step off when you’re out.
  • A zone is eliminated when its last player is out. The last quadrant with anyone standing survives and advances; the other three take the loss. At the 5:00 cap, the zone with the most players standing survives — a tie goes to a 60-second sudden death among only the tied zones.
  • Choose your target — that’s the strategy. Gang up on the strong quadrant, ignore the weak one, broker a quiet truce with a neighbor, then turn on them. All the alliance drama, none of the center-court pile-up. Refs call outs; they don’t police diplomacy.
Roles turn a zone into a team. In both formats, captains assign blockers, catchers, retrievers, and snipers. Once per game a captain may call a 15-second “power play” all-out blitz on one chosen neighbor (no revives). In four-square, a zone that defends its line and picks one target at a time outlasts three zones firing everywhere at once.

Redemption Rumble (so “all teams” means all teams)

The moment a team takes its second loss, it rolls onto a freed arena for a rolling king-of-the-court: the team left standing holds, the next eliminated team rotates in. Track court-holds for a Redemption Champion called out at awards.

  • Rotating variant rules (one per round — the creativity sandbox): Medic (a secret player revives by tag), Protect the VIP (guard one untouchable player), Doctor Dodgeball (team lives while its healer lives), Blackout (glow balls only, if you run it at night).
  • Why it matters: a team knocked out by ~0:34 would otherwise stand idle for 30+ minutes. Here they keep playing 6–10 more short rounds. That is the “max game time” promise kept.

Safety

  • Warm up first. 200 men sprinting cold is how hamstrings pop. The opening brief includes a dynamic warm-up.
  • Four-square keeps teams apart — the big collision risk of an open melee is gone, since each team holds its own quadrant. Still: no crossing lines, no diving into a neighbor’s zone, and call “hands clear” before the opening horn.
  • Foam balls only. Head-hunting penalized (thrower out); above-shoulder hits don’t count against the player hit.
  • Keep the 15–20 ft buffers. Don’t let arenas or courts creep together as the field fills.
  • Footwear + ground check. Walk every court/arena for holes and roots before you start; closed-toe shoes only; hydrate at the 25 / 50-minute marks.
