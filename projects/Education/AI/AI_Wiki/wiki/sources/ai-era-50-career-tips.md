# 50 Things I'd Do If I Were Starting Over (AI-Era Career Tips)

A summarized YouTube talk by a career-switcher (PM to Staff Software Engineer at Meta, now teaching Claude Code / Codex courses) giving ~50 opinion-heavy tips for building an irreplaceable career in the age of agentic AI.
Grouped into five themes: Mindset, The New Type of Engineer, Career Strategy, Money and Health, and Staying Ahead.
The core thesis: **code is becoming cheap, so durable value shifts to judgment, taste, domain ownership, and mastery of agentic tools.** Junior tasks automate first, so learn the tools deeply rather than flee them.

This is the founding source for the [[ai-career]] hub.

## Key claims

- **Read reality correctly.** Your feed is not the marketplace (negative sentiment sells); "never ask a barber for a haircut" (AI vendors are incentivized to hype); demos lie, production tells the truth (cites an MIT figure that ~95% of enterprise GenAI efforts had not shown meaningful progress, while noting it is dated). Only Codex and Claude Code are called out as "survived in production."
- **Value migrates to judgment and taste.** [[own-the-problem-not-the-code]]: deep domain knowledge stays valuable when code is cheap. [[taste-as-moat]]: encoding your taste so a machine reproduces your judgment is central.
- **[[agentic-engineering]] is the new expertise:** context engineering, agentic validations, agentic tooling (building tools for the agent), and compound engineering. Master Claude Code / Codex "like the back of your hand."
- **Learn the layers below** (abstractions are leaky) and **read more code** selectively - read the hard patterns (e.g. the [[react-loop]]) and let AI handle the trivial, test-verifiable code.
- **Learn where the AI fails:** LLMs only ever hallucinate; the useful outputs are "useful hallucinations." Keep a log of where systems succeed and fail (a direct tie to [[evals]] and [[looking-at-your-data]]).
- **Build a [[t-shaped-portfolio]]** (broad breadth + one deep area) - what he believes recruiters want.
- **Career strategy:** ship end-to-end and maintain it; communication is the largest leverage; become worth vouching for (referrals are the best way to get hired); optimize for learning slope, not salary; own the ugly-but-important work; be the bridge between your company and AI; [[build-in-public]].
- **[[cost-of-being-wrong]]:** target domains where being wrong is expensive (medicine, law, regulated fields) - they stay human-led longer; avoid building your identity around cheap-to-automate work.
- **Foundations:** audit expenses, first $100K is hardest, index investing, spend on AI tools (~$200 max plans), rest and train (marathon not sprint), build multiple income streams.
- **Stay ahead:** be an early adopter (he switched everything at Opus 4.5), follow the leaders, bet on yourself, ship while slightly embarrassed, [[build-for-the-model-of-the-future]] (via Boris Cherny), and do not let AI atrophy your fundamentals.
