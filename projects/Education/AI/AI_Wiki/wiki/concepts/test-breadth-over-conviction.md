# Test Breadth Over Conviction

**"We're not Don Draper."**

From [[building-marketing-agents]]: Cody rejects imposing a predetermined message on the market.
The approach is to test broadly and let market response - visible within about **48 hours** - reveal what's actually resonating.

## The common mistake

Founders run a handful of ads, see no results, and give up - **without realizing the same core ad often needs 10-20 variations in positioning** before finding what resonates.

He says he is regularly surprised by which positioning angles end up working.
The example given: for a plugin competing with Yoast, an unconventional explicitly "anti-Yoast" angle might be the one that performs.

## Why this is newly accessible

Historically this level of testing required hiring an agency for tens of thousands per month.
The claim is that a founder can now build the system themselves - with fine-tuning still required - at a fraction of that cost, which is what [[marketing-agent]] describes.

At his stated cadence (2 ad sets/day, 5 ads each), 10-20 positioning variants is a couple of days rather than a quarter.

## Two supporting positions

- **Paid over organic-only.** Both speakers push back on founders who only want word-of-mouth growth, calling it "a rookie way to look at the market."
The argument: paid is the only system where "I put a dollar in and $5 can potentially come out the other side," and you can keep feeding that multiplier at scale.
- **Marketing is continuous, not campaign-based.** Feedback loops are now much faster, so businesses need constant observability into shifting trends rather than a start-and-stop campaign mentality.

## "Volume negates luck," and the trap in it (2026-08-21)

[[one-person-marketing-team]] states the breadth half in one line - **"volume negates luck"** - and then demonstrates the exact failure the phrase invites.

It produced 18 static creatives in a single $3.43 run, plus blank copy-free versions so future promotions can swap new lines onto existing art rather than regenerating.
That last habit is a genuine addition to this page: **separating the copy from the art makes a high variant count cheap to sustain**, because the second campaign's variants cost pennies instead of a new generation run.

But nothing was ever published, no ad was served, and the tracker workbook it built ends with an **empty "angle performance" tab**.
The source proposes the closing loop - connect the agent to real performance data, review weekly, make more of what worked - and never builds it.

**Volume without measurement is not breadth, it is just output.**
The page's original caveat was about *budget* per variant; this source supplies the more basic one about *feedback* per variant.
Twenty variants and no read is strictly worse than two variants and a read, because it costs more and teaches nothing while feeling like progress.

The pairing to hold: generation cost has collapsed to near zero for static creative, which removes the old constraint on variant count.
The remaining constraint is entirely on the measurement side, and it did not get cheaper.

## Synthesis

The transferable claim is that **positioning is an empirical question, not a creative one**, and that conviction about your own message is a liability when testing is cheap.

That is the distribution-side version of [[building-at-the-frontier]]'s central mindset - be empirical, let go of your priors, watch what actually happens rather than reasoning from what should work.
Two sources in completely different domains arriving at "your prior is the problem" is worth noting.

The caveat is sample size: [[leading-metric-over-vanity-metric]] and the ads discussion in [[loops-to-run-your-business]] both insist each variant needs **enough budget for a statistically meaningful read**.
Breadth without sufficient spend per variant is not testing, it's noise, and 20 underfunded variants are worse than 5 funded ones.

For hcho this bears directly on two live decisions.
The Agentic RAG campaign has been running with a small number of message angles; this argues the constraint is variant count, not message quality.
And Rep Today's ASO-led channel plan has kill criteria attached to gates - which is right - but the plan should be checked for whether it tests enough positioning variants to give those gates a fair read before declaring the thesis dead.
