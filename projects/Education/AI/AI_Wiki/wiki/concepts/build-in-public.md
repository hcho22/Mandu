# Build / Learn in Public

The practice from [[ai-era-50-career-tips]] and [[zero-to-hireable-ai-engineer]] of sharing unfinished work openly: it builds tough skin, forces you to finish, and makes you discoverable for networking and jobs.

## Key points

- Sharing work-in-progress is uncomfortable but compounds: open-source projects that "go crazy" can mint staple developers in the new AI ecosystem.
- It doubles as organic networking - "opportunities are attached to people, not job boards" - built genuinely, before you need it.
- Complements the [[agent-saas]] distribution tactic of workflow teardowns and "own one workflow publicly."
- **Start in Month 1, not when you feel ready** ([[zero-to-hireable-ai-engineer]]): "ready" arrives after you start; put every project on GitHub the day you finish it. It is the antidote to mistake #4 in [[career-switch-mistakes]].
- **The honest README** is the sharpest tactic: each portfolio project's README includes "what went wrong and what I'd do differently." Most portfolios fake perfection; an honest one signals the owned-outcome judgment employers screen for - the [[switchers-edge]] made visible.

## Synthesis

This is the strategy hcho is already running with Agentic RAG (build-in-public, waitlist, 5-day campaign), so the concept is less "new idea" than confirmation and sharpening: publish the process, finish and maintain, and let discoverability do the networking.
The tie to [[t-shaped-portfolio]] is that building in public is how the breadth becomes visible and the depth becomes credible to a recruiter or a buyer.
