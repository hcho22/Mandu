# The Four Career-Switch Mistakes

From [[zero-to-hireable-ai-engineer]]: the mistakes that end an AI-engineering career switch happen in week two, not month three, and none are about intelligence - they are strategy errors.

## The four

1. **Starting with theory and math.** You chase linear algebra and backprop, can't build anything after three weeks, feel like an impostor, and quit. Fix: skip it; pick up concepts as you hit them in real projects, where they stick.
2. **Watching tutorials instead of building.** It feels like progress but you watched someone else learn. Fix: the **30-minute rule** - for every hour watching/reading, spend 30+ minutes building with no tutorial open. "A person who builds badly for four months beats a person who watches perfectly for four months, every time."
3. **Learning tools instead of skills.** Go deep on today's framework and you're always behind when the field moves. Fix: learn the skill under the tool (writing a reliable prompt, getting structured output, deciding single-call vs agent) - skills transfer across every tool; tools expire.
4. **Waiting until you feel ready to build in public.** Ready is a feeling that arrives after you start. Fix: [[build-in-public]] from Month 1; put every project on GitHub the day you finish it.

## Synthesis

The antidote is a single posture the whole roadmap is built on: **skill-first, build-first, tool-agnostic, public from day one.**
Mistake 3 (skills over tools) is the most durable idea here and rhymes with [[code-as-sawdust]] and [[capabilities-not-instructions]] - invest in the thing that does not expire.
For hcho, whose learning wiki is itself a build-first, public artifact, this is mostly confirmation - but it is a clean checklist to hand to anyone he mentors, and a reminder that the failure mode is stopping, not going slow.
