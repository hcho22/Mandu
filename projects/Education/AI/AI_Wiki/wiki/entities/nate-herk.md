# Nate Herk

AI automation creator and educator.
Teaches non-technical audiences to build systems in [[claude-code]], explicitly assuming zero prior experience: "You don't have to be technical at all... you just have to know what to ask for."

Author of [[one-person-marketing-team]].

## What he contributes to the wiki

- **[[own-the-context-layer]]** - his central argument, and the clearest statement of it in the wiki: the generation tool is a commodity, the project that holds your context is not.
- **[[three-ps-business-context]]** - Pain, Person, Promise as the minimum context dump before any tooling.
- **[[make-the-agent-report-its-cost]]** - the four-part prompt pattern, and the wiki's only itemized dollar costs for a creative production run.
- **[[agent-interviews-you-for-context]]** - the `grill me` skill as the fix for "I don't know what context to give it."

## Posture

Notable for arguing against his own demonstration, which is why the source is usable despite its weaknesses.
He calls his best UGC output "still very ad-like" and visibly synthetic, notes his aggregator is not the cheapest option, points out that his website was never actually published, and closes with "you can't just expect to throw one prompt out there and then go viral."
He also stresses [[building-skills|building a skill]] only *after* an output comes back the way you wanted it, rather than designing one up front.
