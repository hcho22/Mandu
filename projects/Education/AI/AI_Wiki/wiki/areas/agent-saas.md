# Agent SaaS

The durable hub for building and selling agents as products: the business model, the build ladder, and the trust layer that turns an automation into a product someone pays for.
This area sits at the intersection of the craft (how to build reliable agents) and the career (how a solo builder turns that skill into revenue), so it is directly relevant to hcho's product bets.

## The core shift

Software is moving from "help me do the work" to "do the work with me."
[[agent-saas-sells-work]] is the mental model: you sell a job that disappears, not a tool, and the addressable market is labor rather than software seats.

## Concepts in this area

- [[agent-saas-sells-work]] - sell the job, not the tool.
- [[workflow-with-a-paycheck]] - how to pick a workflow worth automating (5 traits + scoring rubric).
- [[seven-part-agent-spec]] - the spec you capture by shadowing the human before building.
- [[minimal-useful-agent]] - build the smallest useful version; the draft-to-act ladder.
- [[start-as-a-workflow]] - Anthropic's guidance: begin predictable, earn autonomy.
- [[product-wrapper]] - logs, approvals, and a control room create the trust.
- [[outcome-based-pricing]] - sell pilots like labor, then productize the repeatable parts.

## The mirror area: building for agents

[[agent-saas]] sells **agents as labor to humans**; the [[agentic-web]] area covers building **products for agents as the buyers** (identity, wallets, inboxes, agent-readable interfaces).
They are the two sides of the agent economy and share a trust model ([[agent-as-employee]], [[phased-autonomy]]).

## Where evals fit

The playbook's "agent's gym" is an eval set: 50 real examples of the job, scored, and used both as a quality gate and a sales asset.
That connects this area straight to [[evals]] and the [[three-level-eval-framework]] - the same discipline hcho leads with, now as a go-to-market tool.

## Synthesis

One source in, the throughline is that agent products win on constraint and trust, not autonomy: pick one repetitive, paycheck-attached workflow, build the smallest useful agent, and wrap it so the buyer can see what happened.
For hcho this doubles as a solo-builder blueprint and as evidence that eval rigor is a commercial advantage, not just an engineering nicety.
