## Three things worth carrying forward

**1. Epic O reversed a v6 acceptance criterion on purpose.**
US-O03 removed the always-on session clock that US-K01 had shipped as a requirement.
The measurement is untouched (`startedAt` / `elapsed(asOf:)` still feed the log, Default Duration learning, and the Consistency Score); only the display changed, and the total is now revealed once on the completion summary.
Worth noting because the PRD now carries a checked box whose text is deliberately contradicted by a later story - the tracker should not read that as drift.

**2. The Hold Timer bug was a modeling error, not three bugs.**
The hold was written by mirroring the rest timer: same absolute deadline, same restore-from-snapshot.
But a rest genuinely continues while the app is away and a hold does not - the user stopped holding when they left the screen.
Three separate guards were written against three spellings of the same restored-and-already-expired state, and each was a real fix that was not *the* fix.
The rule that ended it: a hold leg never survives the player being torn down, so there is nothing to restore and no door to guard.
Collapsing both timers onto one `Countdown` value type then deleted ~80 mirrored lines and fixed a latent rest-timer bug as a side effect.

**3. The imperial-units story turned into an accessibility and touch-target story.**
The platform `Stepper` measured 46.5 x 32pt, under the app's own 44pt floor in the dimension a thumb is least accurate in.
Three passes at hand-rolled gestures each broke on the same thing - deciding in the view whether a lifted finger is still *on* the button - until the geometry left the view entirely and the control became a real `Button` inheriting the platform's touch slop.
Every defect was found by hosted-render assertions and Simulator screenshots, not by reading the diff.
That gap is what the new `RepTodayUITests` scheme closes permanently: it is the only place a production control is actually pressed rather than hosted.

## The blocker that opened

**Account deletion is missing, and it is a hard App Store rejection blocker (Guideline 5.1.1(v)).**

Audited against the built app on 2026-07-31. The app ships Sign in with Apple with zero deletion path: no "Delete Account", no "Delete my data", no reachable sign-out anywhere, and the Profile tab is still a `PlaceholderTabView`.
Sign-in being optional does not help - Apple treats *supporting account creation* as triggering the requirement.

The teardown capability half-exists as unreachable dead code (`AppleAuthService.signOut()`, `CoreDataUserService.deleteCurrentUser()`, `AuthCredentialStore.clear()`, all called only from tests and mocks), and `SessionPolicyStore` has no delete method at all.
The Keychain identifier survives reinstall by design, so leaving it resurrects a deleted identity - that is the check most likely to catch an incomplete implementation.

PRD is implementation-ready at `01-prd-account-deletion_260731.md`: US-D01 Profile/Settings screen, US-D02 bulk delete methods, US-D03 `AccountDeletionService` orchestration, US-D04 confirmation flow, US-D05 Apple-credential handling, US-D06 optional HealthKit sample removal.
Not started.

US-D05 carries a recommendation worth a decision: with no backend, either (a) surface guidance plus a deep link to Settings → Apple ID, or (b) stand up a revoke proxy endpoint.
The PRD recommends (a) - it matches the "nothing leaves the device" privacy story the App Store nutrition labels will tell, and adds no server dependency or Apple client-secret rotation burden.
