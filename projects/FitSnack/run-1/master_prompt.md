Mission
Take FitSnack from spec to a market-ready package. You are inheriting a real, already-designed product: a discipline-first micro-workout iOS app for busy, desk-bound adults, built around a deterministic on-device engine and an asynchronous AI Programmer, with a full v6 PRD, a locked architecture, and an open naming decision (FitSnack, Cairn, and Stack are all on the table). Your job is not to invent a company from nothing — it is to prove this one deserves to exist, then build everything a founder would need to launch it this month.
Start by reading the existing PRD and positioning work in the repo. Then go to the open internet and pressure-test the thesis against reality: is the pain real, is anyone actually complaining about it right now, is it genuinely underserved, and does "discipline-first, longevity-first, open-and-go" actually differentiate against what's shipping today? Ground every load-bearing claim in real evidence or falsify it. Lock the name. Then design the go-to-market, build the brand and the marketing site, make the launch video and the founder video, red-team the whole thing, and hand me a finished package I could take to market this month. Then prove to me why it would work.
This is a test of how far you can go on your own. I am not going to answer questions mid-run. Make every call yourself, write down why you made it, and keep moving. Within the guardrails below, you have total creative freedom. Do whatever you want. Surprise me. I want to see your best work, not your safest work. The PRD is your substrate, not your ceiling — if the evidence says the thesis is wrong, say so and reshape it.

Guardrails
No new spending. APIs whose keys already exist in .env are fair game. No new paid services, no purchases, no signups requiring payment info, no domain registration, no trademark filings. Check availability — domain, App Store name, and USPTO trademark — for the naming finalists; don't buy or file.
Publish nothing. Everything stays local or in this repo. No deploying to the public internet, no posting anywhere, no emailing or messaging any real person.
Invent nothing. Every quote, stat, complaint, competitor fact, and market claim must trace to a real URL you actually fetched. This applies to FitSnack's own thesis: the "no time to exercise" pain, the discipline-first differentiation, the competitor facts (Keizoku and the strength-led incumbents), and any retention or market-size number are hypotheses until you ground them. If you infer something, label it as inference. If you couldn't verify something, say so. A smaller thesis built on real evidence beats a grand one built on plausible fiction.
Work inside projects/fitsnack/run-1/. All artifacts go there.
Never ask me anything. I will not be watching. Every question you'd ask me, answer yourself with research and reasoning, then log the question, your answer, and why in the build log. Blocked is not an option: if a tool or approach fails, find another route. If a phase stalls, ship the strong 80% version, note what got cut, and keep moving. Do not stop until the definition of done is met.

What "orchestrate" means here
Use multi-agent workflows aggressively. Fan out parallel researchers across different sources and angles — one hunting the pain in real user complaints, one profiling competitors, one on the market and channels. Run tournaments where independent agents pitch competing positioning angles, wedge framings, and the final name, and judge panels score them. Adversarially verify every important claim with skeptic agents whose only job is to refute it — including skeptics assigned to kill the core discipline-first thesis. Use a completeness critic before you call any phase done. Design whatever orchestration the work calls for; these patterns are a floor, not a ceiling.

The arc
Hunt for pain. FitSnack's core pain ("busy desk-bound adults have no time and no discipline to exercise") is a hypothesis. Go to real, current user complaints and either ground it in live evidence or falsify it.
Pick the winner. The business is already chosen, so "picking" here means locking the two open bets: the positioning angle (does discipline-first / longevity-first / open-and-go actually win?) and the name (FitSnack vs. Cairn vs. Stack), each decided on real availability and evidence.
Design the business. Go-to-market, pricing (free core + premium depth), launch channel, and the wedge no competitor owns together — grounded against what's actually shipping.
Build the brand. Complete brand guidelines a stranger could make a new on-brand asset from.
Build the thing. The marketing site plus, at minimum, an interactive prototype of the Ready Screen wedge (open-and-go, zero questions). Runs locally, screenshot-verified mobile and desktop.
Make the launch video.
Make the founder video. Script passes my voice rules.
Try to kill it. Red-team the actual FitSnack thesis — skeptics assigned to refute the pain, the differentiation, and the retention claims. Objections stay visible in the final docs.
Package it. recap.html ties it all together: a stranger understands the business in five minutes, runs the site, watches the video, and walks away convinced or precisely informed why not.

The deliverables list is a floor, not a ceiling
At minimum: a validated pain-and-market thesis with live sources, a locked name with availability evidence, brand guidelines, and a marketing/landing site that runs locally and is screenshot-verified on mobile and desktop, plus the launch video and the founder video. Beyond that, build what a real founder launching this would make and you can make with the tools you have. Ideas to steal or beat: an interactive prototype of the Ready Screen wedge (open-and-go, zero questions), App Store listing copy and screenshot mockups, ad creatives for the top channel in your launch plan, an onboarding email or push-notification sequence, a one-page investor teaser, a pitch deck, social profile assets. Don't do all of them; do the ones that make this company feel most real, and invent at least one deliverable nobody would expect. Every extra goes in the recap page's deliverables map. Quality still beats quantity: one more polished, verified artifact beats three rushed ones.

Definition of done
You're done when a stranger could open recap.html, understand the business in five minutes, run the site locally, watch the launch video, and walk away either convinced or precisely informed about why they're not convinced. Before you finish, grade yourself against this list and fix anything failing: every guardrail held; every claim in the thesis has a live URL; the naming decision is documented with real availability evidence; the site is screenshot-verified on mobile and desktop; both videos render and you actually watched them; the founder script passes my voice rules; the recap page links to every deliverable and every link works; the brand guidelines are complete enough that a stranger could make a new on-brand asset from them alone; the red team ran, attacked the actual FitSnack thesis, and its objections are visible in the final docs; and nothing in the package is a placeholder pretending to be finished work.
