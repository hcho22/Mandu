## The script

I'm Hyung Cho.
I spent ten years as a hardware validation engineer.
My job was finding the conditions where systems fail, before customers found them.

Now I build AI products, and I applied the same discipline to a category with a famous failure rate.

Fitness apps fail on a schedule.
About three percent of the people who install one are still using it thirty days later.
When a product loses ninety-seven percent of its users in a month, that is not ninety-seven percent weak-willed people.
That is a design defect.

I found two failure conditions.

The first is the open.
Most apps greet you with questions and setup, at the exact moment you have no decisions left in you.

The second is the miss.
One skipped day zeroes a streak, and the evidence says a broken streak is where quitting starts.

Nonzero is designed against both.

Open it, and a complete workout is already there.
No questions.
One tap.

Miss a day, and your score dents.
It never zeroes.

Five minutes counts as a full show-up.

The session engine is deterministic, and it runs on your phone.
That means I can test every workout it will ever generate, before you see it.
Ten years of QA taught me not to ship a system I can't test.

Nonzero.
Zero is the only way to lose.
