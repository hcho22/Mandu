# Agents Are the New SaaS

A summarized podcast/tutorial from the Startup Ideas Podcast laying out an 8-part playbook for building an agent business.
Its thesis is that software is shifting from "help me do the work" to "do the work with me": where SaaS sells a tool, [[agent-saas-sells-work]] sells the job itself, targeting the multi-trillion-dollar market for labor rather than software seats.
The playbook runs from picking a workflow, to shadowing the human who does it, to building the smallest useful agent, wrapping it as a product, and selling pilots as labor, capped by a 30-day execution plan.

## Key claims

- **Sell work, not software.** Frame the offer as a job that disappears, not a tool provided. The buyer is offloading a task, not buying capability. See [[agent-saas-sells-work]].
- **Pick a [[workflow-with-a-paycheck]].** Good agent workflows have five traits: they happen constantly, have a clear finish line, touch existing software (Gmail, Slack, Shopify, HubSpot, Zendesk, Stripe), have edge cases that are annoying but learnable, and cause a loss the buyer can feel. Score candidate jobs on frequency, cost of pain, clarity of done, tools needed, and who owns the budget.
- **Shadow the human first** and capture the [[seven-part-agent-spec]]: trigger, context, tools, allowed actions, approval points, escalation, and success definition. "The detail is the product."
- **Build the [[minimal-useful-agent]] (MUA),** not a fully autonomous employee. Four good first versions form a ladder: draft-and-approve, triage, coordinate, act. Cites Anthropic ([[anthropic]]) guidance to [[start-as-a-workflow]] and earn autonomy.
- **The [[product-wrapper]] is what makes it SaaS.** The agent does the work; the wrapper (logs, approvals, controls, pre-launch testing, a "control room" dashboard) creates the trust customers need.
- **Evals are the agent's gym.** Take 50 real examples of the job, mark the right answers, and score the agent; this doubles as a sales asset ("42 correct, 6 flagged, 2 mistakes"). Direct tie to [[evals]] and the [[three-level-eval-framework]].
- **Sell the pilot like labor, then productize** with [[outcome-based-pricing]]. Start with three customers in one niche; charge setup + simple monthly, then move toward per-outcome pricing once value is understood.
- **Distribution = workflow teardowns:** show the painful old way, then the agent way. Sell painkillers, not vitamins; own one workflow publicly.
