# Ship Fast, Learn from Users

The launch philosophy from [[building-5-ai-products-solo]]: ship in days, not months, and learn from real users instead of over-planning.

## Key points

- Launch fast, often within 24 hours; build the whole product rather than an email-capture landing page (cheap now with AI, so demand-testing via a page is a "distraction").
- You build for *your* assumption of the problem; real users hit edge cases you cannot predict, so get outside perspectives as fast as possible.
- Fail fast and recognize each mistake so you do not repeat it - the only way to learn what not to do.

## Synthesis

This is the solo-builder statement of the wiki's recurring "constrain and ship, then widen on evidence" stance: it rhymes with [[minimal-useful-agent]] (ship one workflow), [[build-in-public]], and "ship while slightly embarrassed" from [[ai-career]].
A caveat worth holding against hcho's own strategy: Pigford builds the whole product rather than validating demand first, which is the opposite of the Agentic RAG validation-before-build plan - the reconciliation is that Pigford's products are cheap to fully build and his 25 years of judgment substitute for formal validation. For hcho, buyer-interview validation is the deliberate substitute for that hard-won intuition.
