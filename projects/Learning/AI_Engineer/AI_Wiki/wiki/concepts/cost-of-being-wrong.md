# Follow the Cost of Being Wrong

The strategic lens from [[ai-era-50-career-tips]]: the more expensive it is to be wrong in a domain, the higher the quality bar AI must clear before it can take that work, so those domains stay human-led longer.

## Key points

- Target problems where errors are costly - why medicine, law, and heavily regulated fields resist deep AI penetration.
- Avoid building your identity around cheap-to-automate, low-stakes work (landing pages, front-end scaffolding).
- Corollary: quality and validation matter most exactly where the cost of being wrong is high.
- Its quantitative twin is [[classification-threshold]]: "follow the cost of being wrong" becomes "set the threshold by the relative cost of false positives vs false negatives."

## Synthesis

This concept is the strategic case for hcho's evals/validation edge: high-cost-of-error domains are where rigorous evaluation is non-negotiable, which is precisely the skill he brings.
It also gives a portfolio-and-market filter - prefer building for domains where being right is worth paying for, which aligns with the [[agent-saas]] advice to chase [[workflow-with-a-paycheck|paycheck-attached workflows]] and "follow the money into boring industries."
