# AI Engineer Is a Builder, Not a Researcher

From [[zero-to-hireable-ai-engineer]]: the fastest-growing, best-paid "AI engineer" role is a builder who makes existing models do a specific, reliable job inside a real application - not a researcher who trains models from scratch.

## The definition

- You connect to model APIs, design prompts and context, get structured data out, wire the model to tools and databases, make it retrieve the right information, handle what can go wrong, and deploy it for real users.
- It sits between software engineering, product work, and applied AI.
- **The one-line test:** if you can make an LLM do a specific job reliably inside an app, and understand enough to fix it when it breaks, you're an AI engineer.
- You do NOT need transformer internals, calculus, or backpropagation. That is the research-scientist / ML-researcher job, which is rarer and usually does require the advanced degree.

## Synthesis

This definition is the load-bearing frame for the whole [[ai-career]] area and for hcho's pivot: it draws the line that makes "no PhD required" true and puts the emphasis on shipping reliable systems - which is software engineering plus applied AI plus judgment, exactly the stack a hardware-validation background maps onto.
It reframes the intimidation ("training giant models") as a category error, and it pairs with [[switchers-edge]]: the job rewards making-it-work-and-explaining-why, not credentials.
