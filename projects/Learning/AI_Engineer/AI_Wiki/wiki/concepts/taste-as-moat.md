# Taste Is the Moat

The argument from [[ai-era-50-career-tips]] that as AI makes production cheap, the scarce, durable human value is taste - the judgment about what is good - and the ability to encode that taste so a machine reproduces it.

## Key points

- Fully AI-generated work (writing, images, video, code) is still usually recognizable and often mediocre; the human differentiator is judgment about quality.
- Build taste deliberately: study the greats and the work that inspires you (code, UX, design, "how things should look and feel"), then develop your own.
- Encoding taste for the machine (specs, evals, review standards, style guides) is a central act of [[agentic-engineering]].
- [[running-life-on-codex]] gives the sharpest one-line version, from inside OpenAI: **"the only job left is to understand what you don't like about something and put it into words and tell the AI."** [[jason-openai-codex]] frames taste as the skill to learn now that coding is cheap ("if you want taste, you have to eat" - you build it by exposure), and by "articulate what you don't like" he means specific, actionable critique ("add a tempo keyboard shortcut," "why does it flicker when I switch panels"), not "make it better." He recommends beginners have the agent build something first, then practice being specific about dissatisfaction, rather than learning the vocabulary upfront.

## Synthesis

Taste is the human-side complement to [[evals]]: evals operationalize judgment into measurable checks, and taste is the judgment being operationalized.
For hcho, "encoding taste so the machine reproduces your judgment" is almost a definition of validation engineering, which makes this concept another way to state his edge.
It also sets the quality bar behind [[own-the-problem-not-the-code]], [[dont-let-ai-be-your-architect]], [[slop-free-zones]], and "ship things that last."
