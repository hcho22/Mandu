# The Switcher's Edge

From [[zero-to-hireable-ai-engineer]]: the AI-engineer bar did not drop, it moved - from "do you have the credential" to "can you make it work and explain why it works" - and that move favors career switchers.

## The argument

- PwC's data shows AI-exposed entry-level roles increasingly ask for what used to be senior skills: judgment, communication, and the ability to own an outcome rather than a task.
- A career switcher already has those from a prior career - they have shipped things, dealt with stakeholders, owned outcomes under pressure.
- A 22-year-old with a CS degree usually has not. On the axis employers now weigh most, the switcher is ahead, not behind.
- Make it visible: a portfolio README that says "here's where my first approach failed, here's what I learned, here's how I fixed it" signals exactly that judgment; positioning line "Former [field] who now builds AI systems."

## Synthesis

This is the single most hcho-relevant idea in the source: his decade of hardware QA/validation is not a gap to explain away but the exact judgment-and-ownership the market now screens for, sitting on top of the buildable technical skills.
It is the career-facing twin of the wiki's craft spine - the durable value is judgment and verification ([[taste-as-moat]], [[own-the-problem-not-the-code]]) - now stated as a hiring advantage.
