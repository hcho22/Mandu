# Agent SaaS Sells Work

The founding mental model of the [[agent-saas]] area, from [[agents-are-the-new-saas]]: a SaaS product says "here is a tool your team could use"; an agent-SaaS product says "here is a job your team no longer has to do by hand," and sells that outcome.

## Key points

- The offer is framed as a job that disappears, not a capability provided. The buyer is offloading a task.
- The addressable market is **labor** (human capital, a multi-trillion-dollar market), argued to be larger than the software-tool market because you are replacing/augmenting work, not selling seats.
- The idea-generation test: can I handle this one annoying job better than a junior employee, faster than an agency, and cheaper than adding headcount?
- This reframes pricing (toward [[outcome-based-pricing]]) and product scope (toward the [[minimal-useful-agent]]).

## Synthesis

This is the positioning primitive the whole agent-business playbook hangs on, and it is a useful lens for hcho's own product bets: describe the job that vanishes, not the feature list.
It also explains why [[evals]] matter commercially - if you are selling work, you have to prove the work gets done correctly, which is exactly what an eval set demonstrates.
