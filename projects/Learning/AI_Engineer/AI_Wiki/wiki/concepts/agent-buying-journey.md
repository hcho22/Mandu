# Agent Buying Journey

From [[startups-for-agents]]: agents buy through a six-stage journey, and every stage has missing infrastructure because the old web assumed a human was doing the work.

## The six stages

1. **Finding** - e.g. "find a payroll tool for 40 contractors."
2. **Evaluating** - docs, pricing, APIs, reviews.
3. **Trust** - checking policy, limits, identity.
4. **Transacting** - paying, booking, signing, subscribing.
5. **Using tools** - filing tickets, changing settings.
6. **Recommending** - telling other agents what worked.

## Key points

- The "weirdest" and most novel stage is agents recommending to other agents; Moltbook (an agent social network, acquired by Meta) was an early glimpse, framed as "the tip of the iceberg."
- Mapping the journey matters because understanding the agent's identity, desires, and incentives is what lets a builder create a high-value company - the same reason human funnels are mapped.

## Synthesis

This is the [[agentic-web]] funnel, the agent analog of a human buying funnel, and it is where the area's open question lives: what do "agent analytics" and "agent conversion rate" look like at each stage?
Stage 3 (trust) and stage 6 (recommending) are the least-built and most defensible: trust connects straight to [[agent-building-blocks]] (identity, permissions) and [[capabilities-not-instructions]], and agent-to-agent recommendation is a genuinely new social layer.
For hcho, the trust stage is the validation-shaped opening in the whole journey.
