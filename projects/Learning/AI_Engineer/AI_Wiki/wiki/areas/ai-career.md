# AI Career

The durable hub for navigating an AI-native tech career: how to stay valuable, position yourself, and grow while agentic AI reshapes the work.
This is the "career half" of AI_Wiki, directly serving hcho's #1 goal (income by end of 2026 via a product or an AI role) and his hardware-QA-to-AI-builder pivot.

## The governing thesis

Code is becoming cheap, so durable value shifts to **judgment, taste, domain ownership, and mastery of agentic tools.**
Junior-level tasks automate first, but fleeing the field is the wrong move: the rational hedge is to learn the tools deeply, because if AI could truly erase all engineering, almost no white-collar job would be safe.

## The five themes (from [[ai-era-50-career-tips]])

1. **Mindset - read reality correctly.** Your feed is not the marketplace; vendors are incentivized to hype ("never ask a barber for a haircut"); demos lie, production tells the truth; you are not behind; fear is a crowded trade. Foundations (data structures, system design) make you a better agentic engineer, not a worse one.
2. **The new engineer - what holds value.** [[own-the-problem-not-the-code]], [[taste-as-moat]], [[agentic-engineering]] as the new expertise, ship things that last, [[cost-of-being-wrong]], learn the layers below, read the hard code (e.g. the [[react-loop]]), learn where the AI fails, [[t-shaped-portfolio]].
3. **Career strategy.** Ship end-to-end and maintain it; communication is the largest leverage; become worth vouching for (referrals win); optimize for learning slope over salary; own the ugly-but-important work; be the bridge between your company and AI; network before you need it; pick the manager not the logo; teach to learn; [[build-in-public]].
4. **Money and health - the foundation.** Audit expenses; the first $100K is the hardest; index investing; spend on your AI tools (~$200 max plans); rest is non-negotiable; build multiple income streams; train your body (marathon, not sprint).
5. **Staying ahead.** Be an early adopter; follow the leaders; bet on yourself; ship while slightly embarrassed; luck is preparation meeting opportunity; your first job is not your final job; [[build-for-the-model-of-the-future]]; do not let AI atrophy your fundamentals.

## A named, well-paid role (2026-08-01)

[[fde-in-30-days]] moves the hub from "how to be employable in AI" to **a specific role with a specific method**, and it is the most directly actionable material in the area.

- [[forward-deployed-engineer]] - an engineer embedded inside a client's business to decide where AI belongs, then build and deploy it there. Popularized by Palantir. Stated range: $150K base with equity up to ~$1M for top performers (his characterization, not survey data).
- [[deployment-is-the-moat]] - why the role exists: frontier intelligence is commoditized, so the edge moved to *where, how, and why* you apply it.
- [[two-kinds-of-judgment]] - commercial and technical, and the **best** of both rather than an average. The scarcity that makes it pay.
- [[audit-eval-deployment]] - the engagement loop, with **eval as the load-bearing middle stage** - the artifact that earns a client's trust to grant autonomy.
- [[real-workflow-not-documented]] - the discovery discipline; the documented process is never the real one.
- [[solve-for-the-exceptions]] - "one way it goes right, a thousand ways it goes wrong."
- [[build-on-existing-systems]] - never force a migration.
- [[do-the-job-before-the-title]] - a checkpointed 30-day spec for manufacturing the proof before applying.

**Why this matters more than a fourth generic career source.**
The role's two halves are deployment judgment and eval rigor.
The eval half is hcho's declared edge.
The other half - going on-site, watching how work actually happens, cataloguing every exception, being accountable when it breaks in production - is a description of hardware validation at a customer-facing company, which is what he did for a decade.
**FDE is unusual among AI roles in that it rewards the non-AI half of his background rather than discounting it**, and it straddles both target buckets (AI builder *and* AI evals/quality) rather than forcing a choice.

## The role reframe (2026-08-01)

[[manager-of-agents]] arrives from two unrelated sources ([[running-teams-of-ai-agents]], [[building-marketing-agents]]) and adds a claim the earlier career sources do not make: **managing agents makes you more technical, not less.**
Directing agents through real production problems forces you to learn Postgres, environments, and migrations - and you learn it *by using the agents*.
The carpenter analogy is the cleanest one-line answer to the objection every career switcher faces: **domain skill makes you a better manager of agents, not a redundant one.**

The counterweight is honest: the new bottleneck is **decision throughput** (2-3 high-stakes decisions per day becomes 10-20 before lunch), you will work more rather than less, and it is tiring.

## Synthesis

Two sources in, the hub's message maps almost one-to-one onto hcho's existing strategy: build a T-shaped portfolio, ship end-to-end and maintain it, build in public (the Agentic RAG bet), and lead with the judgment/validation skills that stay scarce as code gets cheap.
The single sharpest reframe for hcho is that his validation background is not a legacy skill to explain away but exactly the "judgment and taste the machine lacks" that both sources say now holds value - and the second source hardens it into the [[switchers-edge]] with market data behind it (degree filter falling, employers screening for owned-outcome judgment).
The two sources agree on method too: the 50-tips talk says "learn where AI fails, keep a log"; the four-month guide says "build, don't watch" and "every prompt change without evals is a gamble." Same spine, career and craft.

**What the 2026-08-01 batch changes.**
The first two sources told hcho his strategy was right in general terms.
The second two make it specific enough to act on, in three ways:

1. **There is a role that wants exactly this profile.** [[forward-deployed-engineer]] pays for the combination of deployment judgment and eval rigor, and treats a decade of watching real systems fail in real operational settings as the qualification rather than the thing to explain away. It should be added to the target list and to the Job-Search Roles wiki (`projects/Job_Search/wiki/`), which as of this ingest covers Applied AI Engineer but not FDE.
2. **The portfolio has a spec, and most of it is already built.** [[do-the-job-before-the-title]]'s four-week checklist is largely satisfied by the Agentic RAG work - agentic system with tools and guardrails, eval harness, leak suite, escalation golden set, CI gate. The real gaps are narrow: an explicit **audit trail framed as a trust artifact**, resumability and failure-handling stated as a deliberate feature, the **three-bucket business framing** (revenue uplift, risk mitigation, cost savings), and Week 4 packaging - **iteration history** plus a rehearsed VP-level pitch. That reframes the flagship as "a product I am validating *that is also an FDE case study*" at close to zero extra cost, which is the highest-leverage move this batch suggests given the job search runs as a parallel hedge.
3. **The pitch has a better sentence.** "I bring validation rigor" is abstract and easy to discount. [[solve-for-the-exceptions]] - "there's one way it goes right and a thousand ways it goes wrong, and I've spent ten years cataloguing the thousand" - is the same claim, concrete and hard to argue with.

The honest counterweight, from [[two-kinds-of-judgment]]: the technical column is well covered and the **commercial column is thin** - cost modelling, incentives, adoption, and internal politics as things actively worked rather than observed. The Agentic RAG buyer interviews are the available reps at it, and running them as free Phase-1 audits ([[audit-eval-deployment]]) would build the weak column while doing work already on the plan.
