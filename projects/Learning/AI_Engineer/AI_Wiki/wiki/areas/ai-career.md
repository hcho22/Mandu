# AI Career

The durable hub for navigating an AI-native tech career: how to stay valuable, position yourself, and grow while agentic AI reshapes the work.
This is the "career half" of AI_Wiki, directly serving hcho's #1 goal (income by end of 2026 via a product or an AI role) and his hardware-QA-to-AI-builder pivot.

## The governing thesis

Code is becoming cheap, so durable value shifts to **judgment, taste, domain ownership, and mastery of agentic tools.**
Junior-level tasks automate first, but fleeing the field is the wrong move: the rational hedge is to learn the tools deeply, because if AI could truly erase all engineering, almost no white-collar job would be safe.

## The five themes (from [[ai-era-50-career-tips]])

1. **Mindset - read reality correctly.** Your feed is not the marketplace; vendors are incentivized to hype ("never ask a barber for a haircut"); demos lie, production tells the truth; you are not behind; fear is a crowded trade. Foundations (data structures, system design) make you a better agentic engineer, not a worse one.
2. **The new engineer - what holds value.** [[own-the-problem-not-the-code]], [[taste-as-moat]], [[agentic-engineering]] as the new expertise, ship things that last, [[cost-of-being-wrong]], learn the layers below, read the hard code (e.g. the [[react-loop]]), learn where the AI fails, [[t-shaped-portfolio]].
3. **Career strategy.** Ship end-to-end and maintain it; communication is the largest leverage; become worth vouching for (referrals win); optimize for learning slope over salary; own the ugly-but-important work; be the bridge between your company and AI; network before you need it; pick the manager not the logo; teach to learn; [[build-in-public]].
4. **Money and health - the foundation.** Audit expenses; the first $100K is the hardest; index investing; spend on your AI tools (~$200 max plans); rest is non-negotiable; build multiple income streams; train your body (marathon, not sprint).
5. **Staying ahead.** Be an early adopter; follow the leaders; bet on yourself; ship while slightly embarrassed; luck is preparation meeting opportunity; your first job is not your final job; [[build-for-the-model-of-the-future]]; do not let AI atrophy your fundamentals.

## The concrete path and the market ([[zero-to-hireable-ai-engineer]])

A second source grounds the hub in a specific job definition, a four-month plan, and current numbers.

- **What the job is:** [[ai-engineer-is-a-builder]] - build reliable features on existing models; no PhD/calculus/transformer-math required (that is the research-scientist job).
- **The switcher's advantage:** [[switchers-edge]] - the bar moved from credential to "make it work and explain why," which career switchers already have.
- **How not to fail:** [[career-switch-mistakes]] - skill-first, build-first, tool-agnostic, public from day one.
- **The path:** Python + plumbing -> LLM APIs -> [[rag|RAG]] + agents ([[single-call-workflow-or-agent]]) -> ship + [[t-shaped-portfolio|pick a direction]] (AI product engineer / applied ML / AI automation).
- **The market (PwC 2026 Barometer, 1B+ ads):** AI-skill roles growing ~8x the market, a 62% wage premium, degree requirements falling fastest in AI-exposed roles; Glassdoor mid-2026 ~$143.5K average ($115K-$181K), seniors ~$285K.

## Synthesis

Two sources in, the hub's message maps almost one-to-one onto hcho's existing strategy: build a T-shaped portfolio, ship end-to-end and maintain it, build in public (the Agentic RAG bet), and lead with the judgment/validation skills that stay scarce as code gets cheap.
The single sharpest reframe for hcho is that his validation background is not a legacy skill to explain away but exactly the "judgment and taste the machine lacks" that both sources say now holds value - and the second source hardens it into the [[switchers-edge]] with market data behind it (degree filter falling, employers screening for owned-outcome judgment).
