# Buyer-interview outreach notes

These follow the one rule from `buyer-interview-script.md`: they ask to hear how the person already handles this, they do not pitch the kit or ask "would you buy."
That framing is what gets honest calls instead of polite yeses.

## Core template (personalize line 1, reuse the rest)

> Hey [name] - [one specific, true line: what they're building now, or how you two know each other].
>
> Quick ask, and you're exactly the right person for it: you've actually shipped client AI work. I'm building a permission-aware RAG starter kit in public - the "who-can-see-what plus is-it-actually-correct" layer for client AI features - and before I build more of it, I'm talking to people who ship this for a living.
>
> Could I grab 20 minutes this week or next? It's not a pitch. I want to hear about the last AI feature you shipped for a client and where the time actually went. If this isn't your world anymore, no problem - even a pointer to someone it is would help.

That's ~5 sentences by design.
Network notes that run long don't get answered.

## Variant A - FourthBrain / bootcamp peer

> Hey [name] - good to see you're [specific: at X now / shipping Y]. Quick one. I'm validating a permission-aware RAG starter kit before I build further, and you're one of the few people I know who's actually shipped production RAG. Could I borrow 20 minutes this week or next? Not a pitch - I want to walk through the last client AI feature you built and where the hard parts really were, access and correctness especially. If you're heads-down, totally fine.

## Variant B - Omdena collaborator

> Hi [name] - it's been a while since [project]. I'm building a permission-aware RAG starter kit in public and validating it with people who've shipped real client AI before I put more into it. Given the work you did on [their AI/ML work], I'd value 20 minutes to hear how you handle the who-can-see-what and "prove it's correct" parts on client deliverables now. This week or next? Genuinely just want to learn from what you've built, not sell you anything.

## Variant C - Past freelance / agency contact (the truest buyer)

> Hey [name] - saw you're still shipping AI features for clients. I keep hitting the same wall on these builds: per-user access plus proving to the client that restricted users really can't see restricted docs. I'm packaging the fix and the evals into a starter kit, building it in public, and I'm talking to devs who live this before I build more. 20 minutes this week to hear how you've handled it, what you reach for, what breaks? Not selling anything yet - I'd rather learn from your last build than guess.

## Variant D - X reply-then-DM (for campaign engagement this week)

Public reply first, DM after they respond:

> **Reply:** Curious how you handled per-user access on that - post-filter, or push the permission check into the query? That choice quietly makes or breaks recall for restricted viewers.
>
> **DM (after they engage):** Appreciated your take. I'm building a permission-aware RAG starter kit in public and talking to people shipping client RAG before I build more. Any chance you'd give me 20 minutes to walk through how you handle access and correctness on client work? Not a pitch, just learning from people who do this for real.

## How to send

- **Send individually, not as a blast.** Personalize line 1 every time - it's the whole difference between a reply and a delete.
- **Prioritize the warmest 5-8 first** (FourthBrain, Omdena, past freelance/agency contacts per the script's source ranking). Target: 3 booked this week.
- **After each reply, log it** under `## Validation` in the daily log, even a "no / not my world" - that's still a data point for the gate.
- **The waitlist becomes your second source:** anyone who checks the call box is pre-qualified, so you may not need to cold-source all 10.
