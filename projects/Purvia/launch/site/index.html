Sharing belongs in the retrieval predicate, not in a filter after it

The usual way to add per-document permissions to RAG is to post-filter: pull top-k by similarity, then drop what the viewer can’t see. The math kills it. If a viewer can see 5% of the corpus and you ask for top-10, the expected number of visible chunks in that result is k × selectivity = 10 × 0.05 = 0.5 — half a chunk. Multi-hop questions that need two chunks become unanswerable.

Purvia pushes the ACL check into the SQL predicate, so the planner ranks only chunks the viewer is authorized to see. Every retrieval call runs under the viewer’s JWT. A workspace tenant boundary sits above per-document sharing, AND-ed into the same predicate — resolved from the JWT, never a backend-passed tenant id.

-- the shape of the thing (see docs/permissions-aware-rag.md §3)
SELECT chunk_id, content, embedding <=> :query AS distance
FROM chunks
WHERE workspace_membership(auth.uid(), workspace_id)   -- tenant boundary
  AND (owner = auth.uid() OR acl_grant(auth.uid(), chunk_id))  -- owner-OR-ACL
ORDER BY distance LIMIT :k;

Illustrative shape, not the shipped SQL — the real predicate lives in match_chunks / keyword_search with SECURITY INVOKER and mirrored RLS.

The chat UI then shows why the viewer can see each retrieved chunk — a per-chunk badge: “via owner”, “via direct grant”, “via {group}”.

The harness also caught us

Our hybrid stack was losing to its own vector leg: keyword recall@5 was 0.140 overall and 0.000 on paraphrase questions, because strict AND matching zeroed any query with one missing word. Fused in, the dead leg dragged hybrid MRR to 0.786 vs vector’s 0.796.

The nightly made it undeniable; the fix (OR-fallback + query-adaptive fusion) was then measured by the same harness: keyword paraphrase recall@5 0.800, exact-token queries 1.000, hybrid recall@5 ≥ vector in every category (MRR still trails vector on paraphrase, 0.767 vs 1.000 — the harness keeps score on us either way).

Before: nightly 2026-07-09, 50-question set. After: nightly 2026-08-27, 60-question set, same 16-chunk demo corpus. We are not selling you hybrid retrieval; we are selling the harness that refused to let us believe in it.

What a run that measured nothing does

It refuses. Empty gold raises a hard error naming the question. A degenerate scale run publishes a DEGENERATE RUN notice instead of a table. A weekly that produces no snapshot is a third state — UNMEASURED — a failed job plus a filed issue, never a green. Those guards are themselves pinned by an offline CI job, because the edit that must never escape a guard is the edit that weakens it.

What you get

The knowledge assistant

Streaming chat over your documents under each viewer’s JWT. Drag-and-drop ingestion (.txt .md .pdf .docx .html), share dialog with per-user and per-group grants, per-chunk “why can I see this” badges, text-to-SQL over an allowlisted read-only schema, web-search fallback, document sub-agents.

The support widget

A white-labeled, embeddable support bot that answers only from documents an owner explicitly published to it — and knows when to shut up. Escalate-vs-answer is deterministic control flow with two fail-closed judged gates; a grounded “I don’t know” escalates instead of auto-sending. Your brand on the widget, never ours.

The eval harness

A 60-question content-anchored golden set you replace with your own; the permission-leak matrix and the no-access replay are derived free from your gold labels. Leak gates hard-fail CI. Nightly and weekly snapshots publish through guarded PRs with three outcomes: green, red, unmeasured.

The stack, readable

Raw OpenAI SDK + Pydantic — no LLM framework. FastAPI backend, React/Vite/Tailwind frontend, Supabase (Postgres + pgvector + Auth + Storage + Realtime). Provider binds per role (answerer / embedder / judge); OpenAI and Azure tested, any OpenAI-compatible base_url accepted.

Measured escalation behavior, current weekly sweep: false-resolve 0% (0/9) against a 5% ceiling; every no-context question escalated (3/3); zero gold chunks leaked to no-access viewers (0/4); and the bytes a no-access customer sees are identical to the generic deferral — so an escalation can’t disclose that restricted content exists.

docs/escalation-weekly/2026-08-27.md. Small populations by design (3/4/9 rows) — read them as a working tripwire, not a benchmark. The LLM-judged leg runs weekly; regressions there carry an accepted up-to-a-week detection latency, mitigated by the deterministic per-PR tripwire.

Who should not buy this

  • You need owner/editor/viewer role tiers. Every Purvia grant is read access; there is no write-permission hierarchy (docs/permissions-aware-rag.md §6).
  • You need nested groups. Groups are flat in v0; a group cannot contain a group.
  • You need proven recall at 100k+ visible chunks per query. Our scale benchmark runs at 10k chunks, where the planner sidesteps HNSW and every cell reads a trivially-perfect 1.000. The instrumentation and the regression alarm for the day that flips are shipped; the curve itself is unmeasured.
  • You want internal search across Slack / Drive / Confluence. Purvia has no SaaS connectors. Buy Onyx or Glean for that job — Purvia is the retrieval layer inside a product you are building.
  • You want a hosted service. Purvia is a codebase you run on your own Supabase + backend host.
  • You expect your corpus to work with our golden set. It won’t, loudly, by design: the example anchors are quoted from the demo corpus, so swapping in your documents means authoring your own golden set — the docs walk you through it, and the leak matrix derives from your labels for free.

The questions an engineer should ask

Your security table is 60 questions on 16 chunks. Why should that impress me?

It shouldn’t, on its own — the README says the same above the table. What should: the property is re-proved from a cold seed on every relevant PR and on main, the gate cannot be configured off, and the harness refuses vacuous passes (a no-access sweep that produced zero cells fails the run rather than passing silently). The denominator is small; the discipline is not.

It failed for 42 days. Why trust it?

The predicate never leaked — the benchmark’s reporting failed while its alarm fired nightly. The fix removed the publish-a-zero path entirely: “measured nothing” is now unpublishable as a number anywhere in the harness. Ask your current stack what its eval does when it measures nothing.

Your own permissions system broke your own eval. Isn’t that the whole pitch failing?

It’s the pitch working in the only acceptable direction. The seeding bug removed the eval users’ workspace memberships, and the boundary responded by hiding everything from them — it failed closed. A permissions system that fails by showing nothing is annoying; one that fails by showing too much is a breach. The part that deserved the embarrassment was the reporting, and that’s the part that got rebuilt.

Is the README table automated now?

No — and the README says so. The hand-transcribed table is explicitly labeled as a deliberate manual step, and it names docs/permissions-scale-nightly/ as the automated source of truth that now corroborates it nightly. What changed is that the automated source can no longer publish an unmeasured number for the manual copy to drift from.

Why not Onyx? It has 40+ connectors.

Different job. Onyx deploys internal search over your SaaS tools and mirrors their ACLs — and its permission-aware retrieval is an Enterprise Edition feature. Purvia is MIT and it is the predicate inside your own product. If your problem is “search our Slack”, buy Onyx.

Supabase’s docs show RLS-for-RAG patterns for free. Why pay?

We build on exactly that foundation, and the core is free too. The guide stops at schema patterns; it ships no eval that proves the property on your corpus, no tenant boundary above sharing, no share UI, no escalation pipeline. The predicate is a pattern. The proof is the product.

Is “single-family eval” proof?

No, and our docs forbid us to call it that: a same-family judge is a weaker proof you must not cite as “proven.” The kit’s default corroborates quality drops across two vendor families (an OpenAI generator judged by Claude — current read: faithfulness 4.83/5, helpfulness 4.00/5, n=60) and tags anything single-judged so a reader can’t mistake it for corroborated.

Is this a support bot?

The widget is one of two surfaces on one platform. Both stand on the same property: per-document authorization inside the retrieval predicate, with a hard workspace boundary above it, kept as two deliberately separate trust models.
