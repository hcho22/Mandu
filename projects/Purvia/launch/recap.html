Purvia go-to-market: the business in five minutes

A permissions-aware RAG platform goes to market on its most defensible asset: an eval harness honest enough to have caught its own 42-day failure — and every claim in this package traces to a file, a line, or a fetched URL.

1. The business

Product: Purvia (name settled, docs/adr/0011-product-name.md) — per-document sharing enforced inside the SQL retrieval predicate, two surfaces (knowledge assistant + white-label support widget), shipped eval harness. MIT core.

Buyer: an engineer adding RAG to a multi-user product. Model: open-core kit — free core (the whole current repo), Pro tier $199 one-time (adoption pack: authoring workbook, deployment playbooks, 12-month prioritized support, theming presets), sold as a labeled founding preorder until its deliverables exist.

Positioning (tournament winner): the receipts, carried by the predicate. Alternatives were scored and their losses became the objection bank — the disqualified one (hosted SaaS) violated ground truth by construction.

The lead asset: for 42 days the scale benchmark measured a real 0.000 under a green hand-copied table, alarm firing nightly. The reporting was rebuilt so "measured nothing" is unpublishable. No competitor will write that paragraph about themselves — ours is in the README with dates.

2. The thesis (falsifiable)

Engineers will pay $199 one-time for a permissions-aware RAG codebase whose security property is proved by a shipped harness, because the alternatives are a DIY pattern guide with no proof (Supabase docs), an internal-search product that paywalls permissioning (Onyx EE — fetched), or frameworks they already distrust.

Kill criteria (one week, from THESIS.md): under 20k impressions/50 engineer replies on the failure-story thread → re-lead with the predicate angle; under 30 signups per 500 visitors → kill the paid tier, keep the portfolio; 5-of-10 inbound asking for Slack/Drive connectors → wrong market, stop; zero preorders despite signups → the Pro boundary is unwanted, one retry then stop. Two tripwires firing kills paid-tier plans this quarter.
