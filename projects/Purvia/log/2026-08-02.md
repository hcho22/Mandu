## What the package actually decided

**The buyer moved.** From "agency or freelance developers who sold a client an AI feature" to "a production-minded engineer, or a 2-5 person team, adding RAG to a real multi-user product."
It also names explicit exclusions: no-code builders, agencies, B2B SMB automation buyers.
That is a real narrowing, and it contradicts the framing still carried in `context/current-priorities.md` and parts of the project README.
Flagged as an open reconciliation item rather than silently overwritten - the launch package is the more recent and better-evidenced read, but the priorities file is the thing hcho steers by.

**The pitch stopped being "adopt my kit."** This buyer's creed is "you don't need a framework," so the package refuses to argue with that.
The line it settled on: you will build the retrieval loop yourself anyway, in a few hundred lines on the raw SDK, and you should; what you will not cheaply build is the proof - the golden set, the zero-leak evals, the attack suite, the escalation gate with a measurable safety ceiling.
Take the kit whole, or treat it as the reference implementation plus the test harness for the one you build.

**The falsifiable core is stated as a bet that can lose:** receipts change adoption behavior.
If eval receipts do not measurably out-convert feature lists for this ICP, the thesis is wrong however good the engineering is.
No market-size number appears anywhere, on the grounds that an unsourced TAM would be fan-fiction.

**The centerpiece story is a failure told straight.** The lexical leg of hybrid retrieval shipped dead, and the nightly harness published that fact for seven weeks across 33 consecutive snapshots before anyone read it.
The fix itself was a boring OR-fallback; what the pass actually bought was instrumentation.
The lesson sold is "publication is not detection," not heroics.

**Pricing came off every surface.** The red team's objection (CAR-4/BR-8): a priced tier with admitted-empty contents reads as "a price tag on an empty box" to buyers and as commercial intent without commercial competence to hiring managers.
Underneath it is a constraint worth remembering - everything currently in the repo is MIT and irrevocably granted, including the deflection widget, the escalation gate, and the eval tooling, and all three are load-bearing for the marketing claims.
So a paid tier can only ever gate things that do not exist yet.
The $149-$249 one-time band survives only as the hypothesis the first 20 buyer conversations exist to test.

## The uncomfortable part

**Nothing has landed in the repo since 2026-07-16 - including the nightly evals.**

The thesis's own kill criterion 4 is "the receipts rot: the nightly series stops publishing, or a security gate goes red without a same-week fix or public annotation, while strangers are reading the artifacts."
The nightly series stopped on 2026-07-16, and the permissions-scale benchmark has been red (0.000 in every cell) since 2026-06-19.

It does not technically fire, because nobody is reading the artifacts yet.
That is the same "publication is not detection" failure the package sells the lesson from - now on its third run, after the 18-night June outage and the seven-week dead lexical leg.
It is also launch precondition 1, and the cheapest credibility item on the whole list.

**Buyer validation has not moved.** Still 0 of the 10 target interviews booked.
The launch package is a much stronger on-ramp than what existed before, but it does not book conversations by itself, and it cannot publish until eight owner-action preconditions are green.
