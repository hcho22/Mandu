_Claims: zebra-validation-ownership, zebra-failure-analysis, purvia-retrieval-golden-set_

The fault was the offline LLM judge. Once it could see the retrieved context, it graded whether a draft was true, not whether it answered the question, so an accurate "I don't have that information" passed. I compared five judge designs on replayed drafts and kept the one that makes the judge name the asked and stated values first. Wrong verdicts went from 38 of 280 to 0 of 300.

_Claims: purvia-e7-escalation-eval, purvia-e7-offline-judge-root-cause_

I treat model choices as experiments with a price. For the live answer gate, a stronger judge model let 15 of 66 should-escalate drafts through on 85 stored drafts, while a stricter rubric on the current model let 1 through, for about $0.00007 more per reply, so I kept the rubric. On retrieval, a 60-question benchmark scores vector, keyword, and hybrid search on pull requests, and when a RAGAS gate flagged a question that retrieved nothing, I traced it to keyword stemming and raised hybrid recall@5 from 0.950 to 0.967.

_Claims: purvia-answer-gate-model-comparison, purvia-retrieval-golden-set, purvia-keyword-stem-tier_

Production data at your scale would be new to me, since Purvia runs on a synthetic corpus, and so would litigation. I'd start by learning how your lawyers judge a good answer, then build the measurements around that. I'd welcome a conversation about how you evaluate your agentic features today.
